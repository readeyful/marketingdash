Content Stack

Three tiers.
One funnel.

Every post has exactly one job. Tier 1 gets you found by strangers. Tier 2 makes those strangers trust you. Tier 3 turns that trust into a client. Before you make anything, name its tier — if you can't, don't post it.

Tier 1 · 50% of posts
Discover­ability
the content that gets you found
Built for people who don't know you yet. Optimized for reach, shares, and the Bellingham algorithm — not for selling anything.
  • Home tours — listings, dream homes, weird homes
  • B-roll of Bellingham life: Fairhaven, the bay, the trails
  • Neighborhood spotlights and local gems
  • Market stats people screenshot and share
Job: reach people who don't know you
Tier 2 · 30% of posts
Likability
the content that makes people trust you
For people who found you and stuck around. They trust your take before they ever need an agent — so when they do, you're the obvious call.
  • Your perspective — takes, opinions, "there, I said it"
  • Behind the scenes of the actual job
  • Client stories and wins, told like a neighbor would
  • The stuff that makes people feel like they know you
Job: make strangers feel like friends
Tier 3 · 20% of posts
Conversion
the content that turns attention into clients
For people who are actually in the game — buying, selling, or about to be. Specific, searchable, and saveable. This is where DMs come from.
  • The questions buyers are scared to ask
  • Searchable answers: "here's exactly what to do"
  • "Stuck? Here's the part nobody tells you"
  • Lead-gen plays, guides, and checklists
Job: give people a reason to DM you
Content Mix
Tier 1 · 50%
Tier 2 · 30%
T3 · 20%

Over any two weeks, about half your posts should be Tier 1, a third Tier 2, the rest Tier 3. If everything is Tier 3, you look like an ad. If everything is Tier 1, you're famous and broke. The mix is the strategy.

The local influencer play is all Tier 1

Tours, B-roll, neighborhood love — that's how you become "the Bellingham guy" people see everywhere. But reach alone never becomes a closing. Tier 1 fills the room. Tier 2 makes them like you. Tier 3 hands them the pen. Run all three, every single week.

Post Formula

How every
post is built.

Every post you make should follow this four-layer system. It's what makes Will Draper's content instantly recognizable, and it'll do the same for you.

The 4-Layer Stack

01
Anchor Surface
Plum, deep plum, cream, or ink. Sets the tone. No photos unless they're high-contrast with a dark overlay.
02
Headline
Anton, ALL CAPS, stacked tight. 4–8 words max. Line-height 0.9. No punctuation on the last line.
03
Green Highlight
Exactly one word gets the green. Underline, box highlight, or color swap. One word. That's the rule.
04
Handmade Detail
Caveat script tag, hand-drawn arrow, or doodle house. Adds humanity. Keeps it from looking like a template.

Voice & Tone

Sound like
Mike.

Not a corporation. Not a brochure. A confident neighbor who knows the market cold — and happens to be great at explaining it.

Direct Vague
"List on a Tuesday, not a Friday" beats "timing is important in real estate." Always give the specific answer.
Local Generic
Name the neighborhood. Name the street type. "A craftsman in Sehome" lands 10× harder than "a charming home."
Honest Hypey
No "HOT MARKET!!!" Real estate is the biggest purchase most people make. Sound like someone worth trusting with it.
Short Long
Headlines: 4–8 words. Captions: 4–6 lines. If you can cut a word, cut it. Tighter copy reads as more confident.
Warm Stiff
You're a person, not a company. "I" not "we." "Mike" not "our team." The warmth is what makes people DM you.
Bellingham Anywhere
Reference real places: Fairhaven, Lettered Streets, Sehome, Whatcom Falls. This is what no out-of-town agent can copy.
Rules

Do this.
Not that.

Keep this open every time you open Canva.

DO Always
  • Use Anton (or Archivo Black as backup) for every single headline
  • Stack headlines tight — line-height 0.9, ALL CAPS, no exceptions
  • Highlight exactly one word per post with the green
  • Keep plum as 50%+ of every composition's real estate
  • Add a Caveat tag or hand-drawn arrow to every post for humanity
  • Sign posts @ridehomerealestate in the lower corner
  • Lead with Bellingham-specific information
  • Use the word "I" — you're a person, not a brand
DON'T Ever
  • Use script or serif fonts for headlines — kills the confidence
  • Make green the dominant color — it loses all its power
  • Highlight more than one word in green per post
  • Use generic "just listed" Canva templates with blue/white
  • Mix more than 2 fonts in any single post
  • Add drop shadows, glow effects, or gradients on the headline text
  • Write a headline longer than 8 words
  • Use "we", "our team", or "at Ride Home Real Estate, we believe"
